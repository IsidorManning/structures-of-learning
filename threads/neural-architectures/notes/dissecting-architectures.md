# Context
In this note I will be focusing on classifying and dissecting neural architectures. This includes understanding their composites, wiring, diagrammatic representations, and uniqueness. My ambition is to cover a large class of architectures, including modern architectures.

The reason for doing this in-depth dissection is to gain a better understanding of not only the architectures themselves, but how they connect to priors. The leading questions are:
- When we express two seemingly different architectures categorically, can we discover something about their structural capabilities that wasn't apparent from the original implementations (and can we verify that discovery)?
- Can we move systematically from architectural syntax to identifying the structures an architecture preserves? To what extent can we recover structural priors from architectural syntax?

Each architecture in this atlas will have a consistent analysis:
1. **Computational dissection:** Original mathematical definition, operations, tensor axes, parameter sharing, and a small working implementation.
2. **Categorical reconstruction:** Its parameterized morphisms, compositional structure, and string or neural circuit diagrams.
3. **Structural analysis:** Candidate priors expressed as triples, with explicit distinction between guaranteed and contingent constraints.
4. **Empirical checks:** Simple tests validating or falsifying the structural claims.
5. **Limitations:** Which architectural properties cannot be expressed without additional  structure?
